Accuracy

Word error rate against a verbatim reference, lower is better. Case, punctuation and number formatting are ignored; every missed, wrong or extra word counts, including spoken disfluencies like “uh” and “mhm”.

About 3 points of every score come from spoken disfluencies and back-channels that live captioning normally leaves out, so no engine can reach 0%.

Caption delay

How long after a word was spoken it appeared on screen. The bar is the average; the white marker is the 95th percentile, the delay you would notice on a slow moment.

Whisper engines confirm a word only after it repeats across two decoding passes, which trades a little delay for stable text. Parakeet waits for a natural pause before it commits a phrase.

How to read this

What was measured

  • Two minutes of continuous, natural English speech, played at real-time speed in 100 ms chunks.
  • Each engine runs the same segmentation and decoding logic the app uses when listening live.
  • Models are downloaded and warmed up first, as in the app’s setup screen; load time is reported separately.

What it does not tell you

  • One clip and one machine. Repeating the same engine varies by about 1–2 points of WER and a few tenths of a second, so treat small gaps as a tie.
  • Clean studio audio. Room noise, distant microphones and accents will move every number.
  • English only. Other languages are not covered here.

Choosing an engine

  • Rapid: fastest captions and lightest on memory; needs iOS or macOS 26.
  • Mix: top accuracy, works on older systems, slightly later captions.
  • Standard / Basic: well under half of Mix’s peak memory, for lower-memory devices; Standard is the more accurate of the two.
