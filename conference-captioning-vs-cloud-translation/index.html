On-device vs cloud

On-device or cloud captions: which fits your event?

By Saamer Mansoor, ConferenceCaptioning · Updated October 12, 2026

A neutral comparison of the two ways to caption and translate a live event, including when the cloud is the better choice.

On-device captioning runs the AI on a computer in the room; cloud captioning runs it in a remote data center. For in-person events, on-device avoids the dependence on venue internet. For remote audiences or human interpretation, cloud or human services can be the better fit. ConferenceCaptioning is an on-device system.

Side-by-side: how the two architectures differ

This compares the two approaches in general, not any single vendor. We have only benchmarked our own engines.

When a cloud service is the better choice

We would rather you pick the right tool for your event.

Mostly remote audiences

If people watch from many locations, a cloud service reaches them without a local network.

No room for equipment

If you cannot place a device and a router in the room, a browser-only service is simpler.

Human interpreters or captioners

Legal, medical or regulated settings may require a certified person, which AI captioning does not replace.

Frequently asked questions

Is on-device captioning better than cloud captioning?

It depends on the event. On-device captioning is stronger when the event is in person, the internet is unreliable or expensive, or you want audio to stay in the room. Cloud services are stronger when most viewers are remote, when you want no hardware on site, or when you need a human interpreter or stenographer.

Does on-device captioning have lower latency than cloud?

It removes the network round trip, which is usually the part that varies. In our published benchmark, captions on a Mac appeared in under a second with no internet needed. We have not benchmarked other vendors, so we do not publish comparison numbers against them.

Where does the audio go with on-device captioning?

Speech recognition and translation run on the organizer’s device, so the audio does not need to be sent to a cloud service to produce captions.

What hardware do I need?

A Mac, iPad or iPhone for the captioning (a Windows PC can use our Chrome extension) and audio from your mixer. We recommend showing captions on the room’s screens. A router is only needed if attendees will also read on their own phones. A second device is recommended as a backup.

When should I choose a cloud captioning or interpretation service instead?

Choose cloud when your audience is mainly remote or hybrid, when you cannot place a device and a network in the room, or when you need a certified human interpreter or captioner for legal or regulatory reasons.
